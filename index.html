Conference Paper: Investigating the systems that enabled the vertical landing of the Falcon 9 first stage.

Abstract

1. Introduction

Vertical landing has rapidly become an almost routine operation, with SpaceX recording 576 booster landings by February 2024, up from 271 booster landings up to February 2024 (Robinson-Smith, 2024; Robinson-Smith, 2026). This achievement is difficult to achieve as the rocket must achieve zero velocity at zero altitude, often without room for error due to lack of propellant (Blackmore, 2017). This paper aims to break down the different systems enabling the Falcon 9 first stage to overcome these problems and land safetly. It will outline the landing sequence, propulsion and aerodynamic control within the rocket, while also considering potential limitations.

2. Falcon 9 landing sequence

We will begin with the sequence of events displayed in Table 1.

Table 1: Main landing phases of a Falcon 9 rocket.
Phase Purpose Main System
Flip Turn stage around after separation to orientate rocket correctly for re-entry. Nitrogen cold-gas thrusters
Boostback burn Send stage back towards landing site. Merlin engines
Re-entry burn Reduce stage velocity during entry into the Earths atmosphere. Merlin Engines
Atmospheric descent Steer the rocket back towards the landsite Grid fins
Landing burn Correct final path, and drastically reduce velocity for landing. Merlin engines + Guidance software
Touchdown Absorb the force of landing. Landing legs
